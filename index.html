Distilling a Jet-Tagging Network into Physics Rules

The JEDI-linear jet tagger, translated into if-statements.

A small neural network sorts particle jets into five kinds: gluon (g), light quark (q), W boson, Z boson and top quark (t). It makes its decision from 16 numbers, its last hidden layer (“jet layer 4”). Here each of those 16 numbers is rewritten as a sum of plain if-statements on physics quantities of the jet (mass, width, how the momentum is shared among the particles, …), for example if mass < 29.64: z -= 0.395 × (29.64 − mass) (neuron 0 of the 624-if-statement formula for 8 particles). The formulas are tuned so that the network’s own last step, fed with them, makes the same decisions as the network.

Each page shows how close the formulas come, what every one of the 16 numbers measures and how the class scores use it, a jet you can try, trees that follow the formula, and every formula as a runnable Python file.
