Distilling the Latent Space of a Real-Time Jet Tagger into Interpretable Physics Equations

The JEDI-linear jet tagger’s last hidden layer, written as if-statements on jet physics quantities.

A small neural network sorts particle jets into gluon (g), light quark (q), W, Z and top (t). It decides from 16 numbers, its last hidden layer. Each of those numbers is rewritten here as a sum of if-statements on physics quantities of the jet, tuned so that the network’s own last step, fed with them, makes the same decisions as the network, while each formula number stays close to the network’s.

The method was run in several setups that differ in which quantities and thresholds it may use or in what the tuning aims at. Each setup has its own page for 8 and 64 particles per jet.

Control: untrained network

Is the good description special to the trained network? The same fits applied to an untrained copy of the network (random weights at each layer’s trained scale, the same number formats). Median over neurons of R² on test jets; every fit is made on training jets.

step 1 on the physics observables (the method)0.890.550.920.16
step 1 on the raw inputs only (pT, Δη, Δφ of the particles)0.580.520.420.08
boosted trees on the raw inputs (a flexible fit, no observables)0.830.640.790.15

The physics observables describe the trained network’s neurons better than a flexible fit on the raw inputs does, and much better than they describe the untrained network’s neurons. The untrained network’s neurons at 64 particles are hard to describe by anything, even by boosted trees on the raw inputs; at 8 particles the observables describe about half of them.

All numbers on the whole test file (260,000 jets) unless stated. “Same class as the network” = share of test jets on which the formula gives the network’s answer.

Model: JEDI-linear (github.com/calad0i/JEDI-linear).
